Ode to Joy

Ludwig van Beethoven, 1824
arranged for D Kurd 9 handpan

Pad numbers

D D3 ding1 A32 B♭33 C44 D4 5 E46 F47 G48 A4

How to read it

  • All numbers sit above the notes
  • Upper row: left hand, plays the even pads (2 4 6 8)
  • Lower row: right hand, plays the odd pads (1 3 5 7)
  • Two numbers stacked, with a D: strike the ding with the free hand at the same time
  • Section A repeats once, then play B
Ready · A twice, then B

Practice plan

  1. Section A, line 1 only. Let each hand stay on its own side: even numbers left, odd numbers right. Say the numbers out loud. Keep it slow, around 60 bpm.
  2. Lines 1, 2 and 4 start the same. Line 1 ends 5 4 4 and leaves you hanging; lines 2 and 4 end 4 3 3 and close it off.
  3. Line 3 is the hard one. The quick pair 5 6 is two half-beat notes. Drill bars 2 and 3 on their own.
  4. Section B adds the ding on the first beat of every bar. Your free hand drops to the centre while the other plays the melody note. Strike both at the same moment.
  5. Watch the repeated notes (5 5, 3 3, 4 4): the same hand plays both, so keep the wrist loose and bounce off the pad.
  6. Bring it up to about 84 bpm, then try a tak with the free hand on the long notes at the end of each line.

Notes on this arrangement

The tune sits in C major (pads 3 to 8), which fits the D Kurd perfectly with no missing notes. In the original melody, the end of line 3 drops to a low G. A 9-note pan doesn't have one, so it's written as 7 (G4), an octave up. On the 12-note, play b2 (bottom G3) there instead.
